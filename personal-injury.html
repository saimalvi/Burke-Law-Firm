Common Questions

Massachusetts Personal Injury FAQ

In most Massachusetts personal injury cases, you have three years from the date of the injury to file a lawsuit (M.G.L. c. 260, § 2A). Some claims — such as those against a city or town — have shorter notice deadlines. Because missing a deadline can bar your claim entirely, it's best to speak with an attorney as soon as possible.

Massachusetts follows a modified comparative negligence rule (M.G.L. c. 231, § 85). You can still recover compensation as long as you were 50% or less at fault, but your award is reduced by your share of fault. If you are found more than 50% at fault, you cannot recover. Insurers often try to shift blame onto you — having an attorney protects against this.

Every case is different. Value depends on factors like the severity of your injuries, your medical bills, lost wages, future care needs, pain and suffering, and the available insurance coverage. We evaluate all of these together so you understand the full value of your claim — not just the quick number an insurer offers.

Most personal injury cases settle without a trial. However, we prepare every case as if it will go to court — that readiness is often what motivates insurers to make a fair offer. If a fair settlement can't be reached, we are fully prepared to take your case to trial.

It depends on the complexity of your case and the extent of your injuries. Some claims resolve in a few months; others — especially those involving serious injuries or disputed liability — can take a year or more. We move your case as efficiently as possible while making sure you don't settle for less than it's worth.

We handle personal injury cases on a contingency fee basis. That means you pay no attorney's fees up front — we are only paid a percentage of the recovery if and when we win or settle your case. If we don't recover compensation for you, you owe us no attorney's fee.
